# Evergreen Care Home — Resident and Family Handbook

Evergreen Care Home is fictional. This handbook was written for the CareDesk project and follows the federal requirements in CMS State Operations Manual, Appendix PP. Where a section rests on a federal requirement, the F-tag is named.

## Visiting

Residents may receive visitors of their choosing at any time of day, and family members have immediate access to a resident, subject to the resident's right to decline a visit (F563).

The front desk is staffed from 08:00 to 20:00, and most visits happen between 10:00 and 18:00, when activities and meals are easiest to plan around. These are suggested times, not limits. Outside front-desk hours, ring the bell at the main entrance and a nurse will let you in.

A visit may be limited only for a clinical or safety reason, for example during an infectious outbreak. If that happens, the home tells you the reason. Please be considerate of roommates during visits.

## Personal belongings and money

Residents may keep and use their own clothing and personal possessions, as space and the safety of others permit (F557). Please label clothing and tell the front desk about valuable items when they are brought in.

If something goes missing, tell any staff member or the front desk the same day. Missing property is recorded as an incident and, if it may have been taken, it is reported as an alleged violation (see Reporting abuse or neglect).

Residents may manage their own money. They may also ask the home in writing to hold and manage it for them. Money the home holds for a resident is kept separate from the home's own funds, and the resident or their representative can see the account records on request (F567).

## Choices and privacy

Residents choose their own daily routine, such as when to get up, when to bathe, and which activities to join, as long as their choices fit their care plan (F561). Joining activities is always optional.

Residents may refuse a treatment or a part of their care, including a shower at a particular time. Staff explain what the choice means and record it, and do not force care on a resident who refuses (F578).

Residents have privacy in personal care, phone calls, mail, and visits, and their records are kept confidential (F583). Staff only share a resident's information with people the resident or the law allows.

## Room changes and keeping families informed

Before a resident's room or roommate is changed, the resident receives written notice that includes the reason for the change (F559). The resident's representative is also told promptly (F580).

The home immediately informs the resident, consults the resident's physician, and notifies the resident's representative when there is an accident that causes injury, a significant change in the resident's condition, a need to change treatment significantly, or a decision to transfer the resident (F580).

Please keep your phone number and email address up to date with the front desk, so the home can reach you.

## Complaints and concerns

Anyone can raise a concern, in person, in writing, or anonymously, without fear of reprisal (F585). The Grievance Official is the Administrator, Daniel Hahn, who can be reached through the front desk (extension 100).

Every grievance is investigated. You receive a written decision that states what was looked into, what was found, and any action taken. If you are not satisfied, you may also contact the State Survey Agency or the Long-Term Care Ombudsman.

## Reporting abuse or neglect

Every resident has the right to be free from abuse, neglect, misappropriation of property, and exploitation (F600).

If you see or suspect abuse or neglect, tell the nurse in charge or the Administrator straight away. The home must report any allegation to the Administrator and to the State Survey Agency immediately: no later than 2 hours after the allegation if it involves abuse or serious bodily injury, and no later than 24 hours otherwise (F609). The home then investigates and protects the resident while it does so.

## Restraints and bed rails

The home does not use physical restraints for discipline or for staff convenience. A restraint is used only when needed to treat a resident's medical symptoms, and the least restrictive option is chosen (F604).

Bed rails are not installed just because a resident has fallen. Before installing a bed rail, the home tries suitable alternatives, assesses the resident for the risk of becoming trapped, explains the risks and benefits to the resident or their representative, and obtains their informed consent (F700). Decisions about whether a bed rail is right for a resident are made by the care team, not by the front desk.

## Billing and payments

Invoices are issued monthly for the coming month and are due by the 15th. Monthly fees depend on the level of care: independent living, assisted living, or memory care.

Billing information is shared only with the resident and with family members the resident has authorized to see it. Questions about an invoice go to the Administrator through the front desk.
